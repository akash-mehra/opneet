## 1. mini01-b01 · Biology · Easy

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Inspiration

During normal inspiration in a healthy person, which change initiates air entry into the lungs?

A. Diaphragm relaxes and thoracic volume decreases
B. Diaphragm contracts and thoracic volume increases
C. Intrapulmonary pressure rises above atmospheric pressure
D. External intercostal muscles relax and ribs move down

**Answer: B — Diaphragm contracts and thoracic volume increases**

Contraction flattens the diaphragm and expands the thorax. Lung volume increases and intrapulmonary pressure falls slightly below atmospheric pressure, so air flows inward.

## 2. mini01-b02 · Biology · Easy

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Gas transport

Most carbon dioxide transported from human tissues to the lungs is carried as:

A. Dissolved CO₂ alone
B. Carbaminohaemoglobin
C. Bicarbonate ions
D. Carbon monoxide bound to haemoglobin

**Answer: C — Bicarbonate ions**

About 70% of transported CO₂ is carried as bicarbonate ions. Smaller fractions travel bound to haemoglobin or dissolved in plasma.

## 3. mini01-b03 · Biology · Easy

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Respiratory volumes

Which volume remains in the lungs even after a maximal forced expiration?

A. Tidal volume
B. Inspiratory reserve volume
C. Expiratory reserve volume
D. Residual volume

**Answer: D — Residual volume**

Residual volume is air that cannot be expelled by maximal forced expiration. It is distinct from expiratory reserve volume, which can be expelled beyond a normal expiration.

## 4. mini01-b04 · Biology · Easy

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Respiratory rhythm

The primary respiratory rhythm-generating centre is located in the:

A. Medulla oblongata
B. Cerebellum
C. Cerebral cortex
D. Hypothalamus

**Answer: A — Medulla oblongata**

The medulla contains the respiratory rhythm centre. Pontine centres modify breathing, while cortical control can temporarily alter it voluntarily.

## 5. mini01-b05 · Biology · Medium

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Alveolar ventilation

A person takes 15 breaths per minute with a tidal volume of 500 mL and anatomical dead space of 150 mL per breath. Alveolar ventilation is:

A. 7.50 L min⁻¹
B. 5.25 L min⁻¹
C. 2.25 L min⁻¹
D. 9.75 L min⁻¹

**Answer: B — 5.25 L min⁻¹**

Alveolar ventilation = (tidal volume − dead-space volume) × breathing frequency. Therefore (500 − 150) × 15 = 5250 mL min⁻¹ = 5.25 L min⁻¹.

## 6. mini01-b06 · Biology · Medium

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Oxygen dissociation

Which combination favours oxygen unloading from haemoglobin in an actively respiring tissue?

A. High pO₂, low pCO₂, low temperature
B. Low pO₂, low H⁺ concentration, low temperature
C. Low pO₂, high pCO₂, high H⁺ concentration
D. High pO₂, high pH, low pCO₂

**Answer: C — Low pO₂, high pCO₂, high H⁺ concentration**

Active tissues have lower oxygen partial pressure and increased CO₂ and H⁺ production. These conditions reduce haemoglobin's oxygen affinity and favour unloading.

## 7. mini01-b07 · Biology · Medium

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Lung disorders

Destruction of alveolar walls in emphysema directly reduces which feature required for efficient gas exchange?

A. Available diffusion surface area
B. Thickness of every alveolar wall by fluid accumulation
C. Carbon dioxide production in all body cells
D. Oxygen partial pressure of the external atmosphere

**Answer: A — Available diffusion surface area**

Emphysema damages alveolar walls and reduces the surface area for diffusion. It does not directly change the oxygen concentration of the surrounding atmosphere.

## 8. mini01-b08 · Biology · Medium

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Respiratory capacities

For a person with TV = 500 mL, IRV = 2500 mL, ERV = 1000 mL and RV = 1200 mL, vital capacity is:

A. 1700 mL
B. 3000 mL
C. 5200 mL
D. 4000 mL

**Answer: D — 4000 mL**

Vital capacity = IRV + TV + ERV = 2500 + 500 + 1000 = 4000 mL. Residual volume is excluded; adding it would give total lung capacity.

## 9. mini01-b09 · Biology · Hard

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Diffusion barrier

Consider these statements about pulmonary gas exchange. I. Alveolar and capillary walls contribute to the diffusion barrier. II. CO₂ is more soluble than O₂. III. Equal partial pressures across the barrier promote net diffusion. Which are correct?

A. I and II only
B. II and III only
C. I and III only
D. I, II and III

**Answer: A — I and II only**

The barrier includes alveolar epithelium, capillary endothelium and the intervening basement substance. CO₂ is much more soluble than O₂. Net diffusion needs a partial-pressure gradient, so statement III is false.

## 10. mini01-b10 · Biology · Hard

**Answer: D — Late distal tubule and collecting duct**

ADH increases water permeability in the late distal nephron and collecting ducts. More water returns to the blood and a smaller volume of concentrated urine is produced.

## 16. mini01-b16 · Biology · Medium

## 17. mini01-b17 · Biology · Medium

Excretory Products and Their Elimination / Counter-current mechanism

The counter-current arrangement that helps maintain the renal medullary osmotic gradient involves:

A. Proximal and distal convoluted tubules
B. Renal artery and renal vein only
C. Glomerulus and Bowman's capsule
D. Loop of Henle and vasa recta

**Answer: D — Loop of Henle and vasa recta**

Opposite flow in Henle's loop establishes the gradient, and counter-current flow in the vasa recta helps preserve it. Sodium chloride and urea contribute to medullary osmolarity.

## 18. mini01-b18 · Biology · Medium

## 19. mini01-b19 · Biology · Hard

Excretory Products and Their Elimination / Integrated urine concentration

Which combination correctly describes urine concentration? I. The ascending limb of Henle is impermeable to water. II. Counter-current flow helps maintain a medullary osmotic gradient. III. ADH increases water permeability in the collecting duct. IV. A highly concentrated medulla alone guarantees maximum water reabsorption even without ADH. Choose the correct set.

A. I and IV only
B. I, II and III only
C. II, III and IV only
D. I, II, III and IV

**Answer: B — I, II and III only**

The osmotic gradient supplies the driving force, while ADH permits substantial regulated water movement across the collecting duct. A gradient alone cannot guarantee maximum reabsorption through a poorly water-permeable duct. Statements I–III are correct and IV is false.

## 20. mini01-b20 · Biology · Hard

Excretory Products and Their Elimination / Hormonal integration

Which combination is consistent with the renal response to reduced blood volume? I. Renin release increases. II. Aldosterone promotes Na⁺ reabsorption. III. ADH promotes water reabsorption. IV. ANF directly reinforces the renin pathway. Choose the correct set.

B. II and IV only

**Answer: D — I, II and III only**

Renin, aldosterone and ADH support recovery of blood volume through pressure regulation, sodium retention and water retention. ANF opposes rather than reinforces the renin–angiotensin pathway, so IV is incorrect.

## 29. mini01-b29 · Biology · Hard

Body Fluids and Circulation / Cardiac pressure and valves

At one instant during a normal cardiac cycle, left ventricular pressure is 90 mm Hg, left atrial pressure is 10 mm Hg, and aortic pressure is 80 mm Hg. Treat the valves as ideal and ignore opening delays. Which valve state and flow direction are expected?

A. Mitral open; aortic closed; left atrium to left ventricle
B. Mitral closed; aortic open; left ventricle to aorta
C. Both closed; no ventricular ejection
D. Both open; left ventricle to left atrium and aorta

**Answer: B — Mitral closed; aortic open; left ventricle to aorta**

The ventricular pressure exceeds both atrial and aortic pressures. The mitral valve prevents reverse flow to the atrium, while the aortic valve opens because ventricular pressure exceeds aortic pressure. The result is ventricular ejection into the aorta.

## 40. mini01-b40 · Biology · Hard

Locomotion and Movement / ATP and cross-bridge reasoning

In an isolated skeletal-muscle fibre, Ca²⁺ has exposed actin sites and cross-bridges have formed. ATP supply then becomes unavailable. Which combination best explains failure of normal relaxation?

A. Myosin detaches normally and active Ca²⁺ uptake continues
B. Myosin remains attached but active Ca²⁺ uptake continues normally
C. Myosin detachment and active Ca²⁺ uptake are both impaired
D. Thin filaments shorten irreversibly while myosin detaches normally

**Answer: C — Myosin detachment and active Ca²⁺ uptake are both impaired**

ATP binding is needed for myosin to detach from actin, and ATP-dependent pumps return Ca²⁺ to the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Removing ATP impairs both processes. Persistent cross-bridges do not mean that actin filaments themselves have shortened.
