## 1. mini02-lw01 · Easy

The Living World / Binomial nomenclature

In the name Mangifera indica, what does indica represent?

A. The family
B. The genus
C. The specific epithet
D. The order

**Answer: C — The specific epithet**

Mangifera is the generic name and indica is the specific epithet. The two words together constitute the species name; the epithet alone is not a complete binomial.

## 2. mini02-lw02 · Easy

The Living World / Taxonomic hierarchy

Which taxonomic category lies immediately above genus in the standard hierarchy?

A. Family
B. Order
C. Species
D. Class

**Answer: A — Family**

The ascending sequence is species, genus, family, order, class, phylum or division, and kingdom. A family groups related genera.

## 3. mini02-lw03 · Easy

The Living World / Nomenclature conventions

Which rule is correct for a scientific binomial printed in a textbook?

A. Both words begin with capitals
B. The genus begins with a capital and the specific epithet with a lowercase letter; both are italicised
C. Only the specific epithet is italicised
D. The genus is always written after the specific epithet

**Answer: B — The genus begins with a capital and the specific epithet with a lowercase letter; both are italicised**

The generic name begins with a capital letter and the specific epithet with a lowercase letter. Both are italicised in print; when handwritten, the words are underlined separately.

## 4. mini02-lw04 · Easy

The Living World / Taxon and category

Which option names a taxon rather than just a rank in the taxonomic hierarchy?

A. Family
B. Order
C. Genus
D. Mammalia

**Answer: D — Mammalia**

Mammalia is a particular group of organisms, hence a taxon, at the rank of class. Family, order and genus name ranks without specifying a particular group.

## 5. mini02-lw05 · Medium

The Living World / Shared taxonomic ranks

Two species are placed in the same genus. Which category must they also share in the standard hierarchy?

A. Specific epithet
B. Family
C. Subspecies
D. Population

**Answer: B — Family**

Species within one genus necessarily belong to the same family and all higher categories. They need not have the same specific epithet or belong to one population.

## 6. mini02-lw06 · Medium

The Living World / Increasing inclusiveness

Choose the sequence with progressively more inclusive taxonomic categories.

A. Genus → species → family → order
B. Family → genus → order → class
C. Species → genus → family → order
D. Order → family → genus → species

**Answer: C — Species → genus → family → order**

Moving from species through genus, family and order includes a broader range of organisms. Shared specific features generally decrease as inclusiveness increases.

## 7. mini02-lw07 · Medium

Which description best distinguishes systematics from simply assigning names to organisms?

A. It considers diversity and evolutionary relationships
B. It excludes identification and classification

**Answer: A — It considers diversity and evolutionary relationships**

Systematics studies organismal diversity and relationships, including evolutionary relationships. Naming, identification and classification contribute to this broader task.

## 8. mini02-lw08 · Medium

The Living World / Metabolism

A cell-free extract carries out a metabolic reaction in a test tube. Which conclusion is appropriate?

A. The test tube is a living organism
B. All non-living objects have metabolism
C. Metabolism cannot occur outside an intact organism
D. A reaction can occur in vitro without the experimental system being a living organism

**Answer: D — A reaction can occur in vitro without the experimental system being a living organism**

Enzymes from cells can catalyse reactions in vitro. Demonstrating such a reaction does not give the cell-free system the integrated cellular organisation of a living organism.

## 9. mini02-lw09 · Medium

The Living World / Taxonomic similarity

Members of which pair would generally share more specific taxonomic characters?

A. Two species of one genus
B. Two genera of one family
C. Two families of one order

**Answer: A — Two species of one genus**

A genus is less inclusive than a family, order or class. Species within a genus therefore generally share more specific characters than members compared only at higher ranks.

## 10. mini02-lw10 · Hard

B. I and II only

## 11. mini02-lw11 · Hard

The Living World / Hierarchical inference

Species P and Q belong to different genera in the same family; species R belongs to a different family in the same order. Which inference follows from this information?

A. P and Q must be capable of interbreeding
B. P and R must belong to different classes
C. P, Q and R share an order, while P and Q additionally share a family
D. Q and R must share a genus

**Answer: C — P, Q and R share an order, while P and Q additionally share a family**

A family is nested within an order, and the order within a class. All three share the order; P and Q additionally share the family. Membership of the same family does not establish interbreeding.

## 27. mini02-bc15 · Hard

In a fungal sexual cycle with a dikaryotic phase, which order is correct?

C. Plasmogamy → dikaryotic phase → karyogamy → meiosis
D. Plasmogamy → meiosis → dikaryotic phase → karyogamy

**Answer: C — Plasmogamy → dikaryotic phase → karyogamy → meiosis**

## 49. mini02-br01 · Easy

Breathing and Exchange of Gases / Diffusion direction

Under normal conditions, oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into pulmonary blood mainly because:

A. Alveolar pO₂ exceeds the pO₂ of incoming deoxygenated blood

C. Oxygen is actively pumped across the alveolar wall

**Answer: A — Alveolar pO₂ exceeds the pO₂ of incoming deoxygenated blood**
